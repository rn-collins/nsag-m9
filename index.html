Why This Module Exists

Cannabis use is common, increasingly legal, and pharmacologically active — it is metabolized through the CYP450 enzyme system that also governs many common prescription drugs, which means undocumented use is a live drug-interaction risk, not a lifestyle footnote. The gap this module is built around is specific: roughly 35% of patients report cannabis use when asked confidentially, while under 5% of patient charts document it. That gap is not a patient-honesty problem; it is a governance problem, and it sits across six points in the clinical pathway.

An institution closes the gap when it uses a validated screening instrument instead of an ad hoc question (Screening Instruments), when its EHR has a structured field for the answer (EHR Documentation Infrastructure), when its clinicians are trained on the relevant pharmacology (Provider Training & Competency), when interaction risk is flagged rather than left to individual recall (Drug Interaction Governance), when patients have a safe context to disclose in the first place (Disclosure Safety Architecture), and when someone is tracking the documentation rate as a quality metric (Quality Improvement Tracking). An institution that governs all six converts a confidential disclosure into a clinical fact the next provider can act on.
Last reviewed August 2026. Cannabis legal status, scheduling, and clinical guidance continue to shift by jurisdiction — confirm current regulatory and pharmacological guidance with your institution's clinical and legal counsel before adopting policy language.
